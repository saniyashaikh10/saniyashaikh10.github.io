Case Study 01

End-to-End Sales Analytics

Furniture looked like a strong revenue category. It wasn't a strong profit category — here's how I found out why, how long it's been happening, and where it's worst.

Python pandas EDA Statistics
01

Business Problem

Furniture was generating strong revenue, but nobody had actually questioned whether it was profitable revenue. I set out to check — and to find out whether the problem was new, isolated to one region, or something deeper.

02

Approach

  • Retail sales dataset (Kaggle "Sample Superstore"), ~10,000 orders across 3 years / 16 quarters
  • Grouped and compared sales, profit, and discount rate by product category, sub-category, region, and time period using pandas
  • Quantified the discount-margin relationship with a Pearson correlation, rather than relying on a visual trend alone
03

Key Findings

  • Furniture generates £742k in sales but only a 2.5% profit margin — far behind Office Supplies (17.0%) and Technology (17.4%)
  • Tables has posted a negative margin in all 16 quarters from 2014–2017 — this is a structural issue, not a recent decline
  • Losses are concentrated in the East region (−14.7% margin) and specific states — Pennsylvania, Illinois, and Ohio each lose over 40% margin on Tables and Bookcases
  • The discount-margin relationship is statistically strong: r = −0.93 within Tables specifically, confirming discounting as the primary driver rather than a coincidence
04

Where the losses are

Profit margin by sub-category — Tables and Bookcases are the only categories consistently in the red.

05

Has this always been a problem?

Quarterly margin, 2014–2017. Tables (maroon) never crosses into positive territory — this isn't a new or worsening trend, it's been this way the whole time.

06

Where are the losses concentrated?

Margin on Tables + Bookcases by region. Every region loses money, but East is worst by a wide margin.

At the state level, the problem is even sharper: Pennsylvania (−41.3%), Illinois (−44.9%), and Ohio (−40.9%) are the worst-hit — a small, specific list worth investigating first rather than a broad regional rollout.

07

Why: discounting eats the margin

Each bubble is a sub-category — bubble size shows sales volume. Bubbles on the right sit at higher discount levels; the ones below the line are losing money.

The visual pattern holds up statistically: the correlation between discount and margin within Tables alone is r = −0.93 — a very strong negative relationship, not just a suggestive scatter.

08

Recommendation

Cap or restructure discounting specifically on Tables and Bookcases rather than applying blanket furniture discounts, starting with Pennsylvania, Illinois, and Ohio where the losses are most severe. Investigate why reps in these states discount so heavily — competitive pressure, aging stock, or a pricing structure issue — before assuming discounting itself is the problem, since categories like Binders show heavy discounting doesn't automatically erase margin.
